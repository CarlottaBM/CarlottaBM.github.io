Carlotta Barone-MacDonald

What will it take for quantum computation to become a useful tool for understanding nature?

I was drawn to quantum mechanics because it offers an elegant, counterintuitive, and philosophically strange description of nature. What now motivates me is the possibility that quantum systems could help us model, simulate, and reason about physical systems in ways that are difficult or impossible classically. My work asks what stands between that promise and practical quantum technologies. I explore the technical and institutional bottlenecks that shape progress: from algorithms, hardware, and problem selection to validation, funding, and field-building.

This website is an attempt at collecting the questions, projects and research directions shaping my work. Please explore at your leisure, and feel free to reach out if you want to chat about any of it!

About me

a little about me

Hi there! I’m Carlotta Barone-MacDonald, a master’s student at MIT pursuing a dual degree in Technology & Policy and Electrical Engineering & Computer Science, with a background in mathematics and physics from École Polytechnique in France.

My path has moved between theory, experiment, and science policy. I have worked on quantum information science, quantum resources, experimental systems, and questions about how emerging scientific fields gain credibility.

I am especially interested in research that combines mathematical modeling, hands-on systems, and a broader understanding of how technologies become useful in the world.
