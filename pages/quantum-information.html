Quantum theory, algorithms, and resources

Quantum Information

What makes a quantum system useful? or more useful than it's classical counterpart. Some quantum states and circuits can still be efficiently simulated with classical systems. In that case, what is quantum advantage? How do we quantify it? How do we harness it in real systems?

I am especially interested in the boundary between quantum and classical descriptions. When does a quantum system become hard to simulate? Which resources make that possible? And what can we learn by comparing quantum systems to their closest classical analogues?

Research themes

Quantifying quantum advantage

Resource theory gives a language for identifying what makes a quantum state or operation useful. I am interested in resources such as magic, entanglement, coherence, and delocalization, and in how they relate to computational advantage.

Stabilizer entropy project →

Classical analogues and simulation limits

Another direction I want to explore is the comparison between quantum systems and classical analogues. This includes asking what features make a system genuinely quantum, what can be reproduced classically, and where efficient classical simulation breaks down.

Delocalization project →

Quantum algorithms and simulation

I am interested in how quantum algorithms generate, transform, and use quantum resources. Simulation tools help connect the circuit-level view of algorithms to the underlying state dynamics.

Algorithms and simulation →

Questions I want to pursue

  • What makes a quantum system hard to simulate classically?
  • How can we quantify quantum advantage in a way that is physically meaningful?
  • Which resources are generated during a quantum computation?
  • How do noise, measurement constraints, and hardware limits affect those resources?
  • What can classical analogues teach us about the boundary between classical and quantum behavior?
