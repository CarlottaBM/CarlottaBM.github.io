Quantum Information & Resources

What makes a quantum system useful for computation? Not every quantum state or circuit gives an advantage over its classical counterpart. I am interested in the resources, structures, and limits that determine when quantum systems become hard to simulate and potentially useful.

This direction connects my work on stabilizer entropies, magic, quantum algorithms, and the boundary between quantum and classical descriptions. It asks what resources are generated during computation, how they can be quantified, and how they relate to practical quantum advantage.

Questions for future work

How do quantum resources evolve under noise, measurement, and hardware constraints? Which resource diagnostics are useful for evaluating near-term quantum systems?

Questions I want to pursue

  • What makes a quantum system hard to simulate classically?
  • Which resources make quantum computation powerful?
  • How do magic, entanglement, coherence, and delocalization relate to useful quantum advantage?
  • How can resource diagnostics be connected to physical implementation and experiment?
