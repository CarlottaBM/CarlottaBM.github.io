About

About me.

My interests lie at the intersection of quantum science, engineering, and policy. Please explore this page to dive into my path through quantum mechanics, quantum engineering and the questions that motivate my research, and collaborators who help shape the work.

Background

I’m Carlotta Barone-MacDonald, a second-year master’s student at MIT pursuing a dual Master's degree in Technology and Policy and Electrical Engineering, with abackground in mathematics and physics from École Polytechnique in France.

I grew up in France and was trained in a system that places equal emphasis on mathematical rigor and scientific breadth. At École Polytechnique, I developed a deep foundation in theoretical physics and quantitative reasoning, which later led me to research in quantum information science, studying quantum resources, experimental claims, and the structure of emerging quantum technologies.

Research question

Over time, my interests expanded beyond individual experiments toward a broader question: how do scientific fields transition from promising physics to infrastructure and institutional commitment?

That question now defines my work at the intersection of quantum engineering and policy.

Making and experimentation

Alongside research, I care deeply about building and making. At MIT, courses like How to Make (Almost) Anything strengthened my appreciation for fabrication, prototyping, and hands-on experimentation.

Bonus: Creative work

Outside the lab, I write poetry and am drawn to many creative forms of expression!

Whether through physics, policy, or making, I am motivated by intellectual honesty, long-term thinking, and a desire to help shape technological futures responsibly.

Team & collaborators

My research has developed through conversations across quantum engineering, nuclear science, materials, and technology policy. I am especially grateful to the advisors, collaborators, and research communities who have helped me refine the questions I ask and the standards of evidence I use.
