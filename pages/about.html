A little more about me.

How'd I get into Quantum?

My interest in quantum began with quantum mechanics itself: its counterintuitive ideas, its mathematical elegance, and the philosophical questions it raises about how we understand the physical world. Growing up in France and studying mathematics and physics at École Polytechnique gave me the rigorous foundation to pursue those questions more deeply.

That curiosity led me to quantum information science. My work has explored quantum resources, algorithms and simulation, experimental claims, and the technical bottlenecks between promising physics and useful quantum technologies. At MIT, where I am pursuing master’s degrees in Electrical Engineering and in Technology and Policy, I have also begun asking how funding, validation, infrastructure, and institutions shape what becomes possible.

The question connecting this work is simple to state and difficult to answer: what will it take for quantum computation to become a useful tool for understanding nature?

Carlotta beyond the lab.

Outside of research, I write poetry and find in music another way of thinking through rhythm, structure, and emotion. I am drawn to philosophy for the questions it keeps open, and to languages for the different ways they make it possible to see and describe the world.

I also love sailing—the combination of attention, movement, and responsiveness to forces larger than oneself. These interests may sit outside my formal work, but they shape the curiosity and sense of perspective I bring to it.

And I care deeply about making things. Hands-on experimentation and fabrication, including my work in MIT’s How to Make (Almost) Anything, offer a welcome counterpoint to abstract questions: an idea has to meet the material world.
