Scientific Computing & ML

I use computation as a bridge between mathematical models and physical systems. This includes numerical simulation, physics-informed modeling, uncertainty analysis, and machine-learning tools for scientific data.

This direction collects coursework and research projects where the main contribution is computational: building models, testing assumptions, implementing algorithms, and using code to reason about physical behavior.

Selected projects

Physics-Informed Neural Networks

Coursework on physics-informed neural networks for neutron transport, including trial functions, loss construction, and boundary-dependent model design.

Materials and Nuclear Engineering ML

Machine-learning coursework involving scientific representations, physical constraints, and model-building for nuclear science and engineering problems.

Computational Notebooks for Physical Systems

Jupyter-based modeling workflows for circuits, detector geometry, uncertainty propagation, and other physical systems.

Questions I want to pursue

  • How can computational models help us reason about physical systems before or alongside experiments?
  • How can ML be made more physically interpretable and useful for scientific workflows?
  • How should uncertainty, constraints, and domain knowledge be encoded in computational tools?
