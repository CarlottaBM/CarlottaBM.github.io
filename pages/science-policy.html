Science policy and field building

How do we know which quantum technologies are real breakthroughs — and which are hype?

My thesis research studies how emerging quantum fields gain credibility, funding, and institutional support before their long-term value is fully known.

Project title

Evaluating Emerging Quantum Technologies Under Uncertainty

A research project at the intersection of quantum engineering, science policy, and field-building.

Cases

Nuclear quantum optics and quantum batteries, using quantum computing as a more mature reference field.

Problem

Emerging fields are evaluated before evidence is settled.

Frontier research programs often face ambiguous experimental signals, contested validation standards, and unclear scaling paths. The project asks how funders and institutions can distinguish promising uncertainty from hype.

Approach

A comparative map of credibility, evidence, and funding.

I compare technical evidence with institutional signals: reproducibility, calibration, publication patterns, funding concentration, standards activity, talent pipelines, and field narratives.

Technical indicators

Measurement rigor, reproducibility, noise characterization, benchmarks, and scalability assumptions.

Institutional indicators

Funding patterns, organizations, standards formation, field-building activity, and policy narratives.

Methods

Case studies, bibliometrics, interviews, expert surveys, and comparative field analysis.

Output

A framework for deciding whether to explore, validate, scale, or pause a research direction.

Contribute a perspective

I welcome case examples, datasets, interview perspectives, and critiques of the framework.

Why it matters

Funding choices shape which quantum futures become possible.

Better evaluation frameworks can make credibility judgments more explicit and reduce both hype-driven overcommitment and premature dismissal of promising fields.
