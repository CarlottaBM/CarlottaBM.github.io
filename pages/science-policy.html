Science Policy & Field Building

How do new scientific fields become credible?

Emerging technologies attract attention, talent, and funding before their long-term value is clear. How should institutions decide which fields to explore, validate, or scale?

I study how technical evidence and institutional choices interact while a field is still taking shape. My interests sit at the intersection of quantum engineering, innovation, and science policy.

Field building

Scientific fields do not grow through discoveries alone. Funding programs, research communities, shared standards, roadmaps, and compelling narratives all influence which ideas receive sustained attention.

I am especially interested in the feedback between these institutional structures and the evidence a field produces: communities shape what gets measured, while new results reshape the community’s priorities and claims.

My research at MIT

At the MIT Industrial Performance Center, I research how emerging quantum fields gain credibility and institutional support under uncertainty. I compare technical signals—such as reproducibility, benchmarks, and scaling assumptions—with funding patterns, organizations, and the narratives used to define a field’s promise.

My current work examines developing areas of quantum science alongside more established fields. The goal is to understand how funders and research institutions can support experimentation without allowing hype to substitute for evidence.

Why it matters

Decisions made early in a field’s development shape which questions are pursued and which technologies become possible. More explicit evaluation can help institutions remain open to promising ideas while still demanding rigorous evidence.
