Science policy and field building

When is a quantum technology ready to build?

We need to evaluate technologies before they work, but we often lack objective criteria for deciding when early evidence is strong enough to justify major infrastructure, standardization, and funding commitments.

My approach

  • Case studies of emerging fields: quantum computing, quantum batteries, and nuclear quantum optics.
  • Data on funding trends, publications, bibliometrics, expert surveys, and stakeholder interviews.
  • A framework and metric for evaluating emerging quantum fields by mapping funding and credibility dynamics.

Research output

A framework for evaluating emerging quantum fields: mapping how scientific credibility forms, how funding concentrates, and how technical evidence becomes a basis for long-term commitment.

Abstract

From experimental demonstrations to engineering commitments

Quantum technologies promise computational and sensing capabilities beyond classical limits, yet large-scale infrastructure must be built before practical advantage is fully demonstrated. Early funding and standardization decisions therefore determine which quantum architectures mature, and which do not.

This project studies how experimental results transition from scientific demonstrations to engineering commitments. By comparing research communities working on related physics under different institutional conditions, I identify which technical signals — reproducibility, calibration practices, noise behavior, and measurement standards — meaningfully indicate scalability.

Rather than asking whether an experiment is correct, this research asks: when is evidence strong enough to justify building infrastructure around it? The goal is to develop clearer evaluation frameworks that support better funding, standardization, and industrialization decisions in quantum engineering.

Motivation

Why funding concentration matters

Investment in quantum computing has grown rapidly over the past decade, shaping infrastructure and talent allocation across the field. This matters because funding does not only follow technical promise; it also helps determine which communities, standards, architectures, and institutions become durable.

Research questions

Emerging fields lead to emerging questions

  • How do emerging quantum fields transition from exploratory science to infrastructure investment?
  • Which technical indicators meaningfully predict scalability?
  • How do institutional and funding structures shape these transitions?

Proposed method

Comparing scientific ecosystems

I approach emerging quantum fields as scientific ecosystems: communities with technical claims, experimental practices, institutions, funding channels, and standards. The aim is to map how those elements interact before a field has fully converged on its benchmarks or applications.

Comparative case studies

Compare research programs such as nuclear quantum optics and quantum batteries to understand different pathways from exploratory science to institutional commitment.

Funding and institutional patterns

Analyze how funding, organizations, and infrastructure shape the credibility and direction of emerging quantum fields.

Experimental evidence standards

Examine reproducibility, calibration practices, measurement standards, and noise behavior as possible indicators of scalability.

Expert and stakeholder interviews

Use interviews with researchers and decision-makers to understand how credibility judgments are made in practice.

Mapping work

Looking at different scientific ecosystems

One goal of the project is to make a map of scientific ecosystems: not just what a field claims, but how evidence, funding, institutions, and technical standards interact over time.

Application

Applying this to contemporary research programs

The framework can be applied to contemporary research programs to compare how different fields accumulate evidence, attract attention, and move toward infrastructure commitments. The goal is not to rank fields simplistically, but to make the assumptions behind investment and credibility judgments more explicit.

Intended impact

Better tools for funding uncertain science

This work aims to provide tools that help scientists, funders, and policymakers distinguish exploratory demonstrations from technologies ready for long-term development. By clarifying how evidence translates into commitment, the research supports more deliberate capital allocation in quantum engineering.
