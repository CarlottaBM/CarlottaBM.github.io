Delocalization as a Resource

This page will describe my bachelor thesis work on delocalized quantum states in quantum annealing and exciton diffusion. The broader question was how delocalization can act as a useful physical resource for transport, optimization, and energy transfer.

This page will eventually include a fuller description of the thesis, including the physical motivation, the quantum and classical random walk models, the connection to light-harvesting systems, and the analogy with quantum annealing.

Project summary

In this project, I compared quantum and classical descriptions of transport. I studied how delocalized quantum states can support motion across an energy or graph landscape, and how this idea appears both in quantum annealing and in exciton diffusion in light-harvesting systems.

The project connected several ideas that continue to shape my interests: quantum walks, optimization, open-system dynamics, and the role of quantum structure in physical processes that may be difficult to capture with purely classical models.

Connection to quantum and analog simulation

This project fits into my broader interest in quantum information science because it treats a physical property — delocalization — as a possible computational or dynamical resource. It also connects theory to physical systems, which is the direction I want much of my future work to take.
