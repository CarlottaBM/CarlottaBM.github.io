Quantum & Analogue Simulation

I am interested in simulation as a way of understanding physical systems, whether the simulator is quantum, classical, or computational. This includes quantum models of transport and energy transfer, as well as classical analog systems that reproduce quantum-like dynamics.

This direction connects my bachelor thesis on exciton diffusion and light-harvesting systems with newer work on coupled oscillators as analog models. The common thread is the use of simplified physical systems to study transport, coupling, resonance, delocalization, and the boundary between classical and quantum behavior.

Coupled Oscillator Analog Models

Modeling coupled LC oscillators as a classical analog of quantum-like dynamics, including resonance, mode coupling, driven response, and energy transfer.

Quantum Walks and Transport

Using quantum and classical walk models to compare transport processes and identify where quantum structure changes the dynamics.

Questions I want to pursue

  • What physical problems are naturally suited to quantum simulation?
  • What can classical analogs teach us about quantum dynamics?
  • How do coupling, resonance, and delocalization shape transport in physical systems?
  • Where do classical models stop being useful, and where do quantum models become necessary?
